## Section A — Comprehension (Water A1.1)

Instruction: *Pick the best answer. It's OK if you're unsure — guess from what you saw in the game.*

### Q1. Molecule polarity

**In a water (H₂O) molecule, which statement is most accurate?**

- [ ] Oxygen and the two hydrogens share electrons evenly, so the molecule has no charged ends.  
- [x] **Oxygen pulls shared electrons more strongly, so the molecule has slightly negative (O) and slightly positive (H) ends — it is polar.**  
- [ ] The hydrogens are negative and oxygen is positive because hydrogen is heavier.  
- [ ] Water molecules only become polar when they freeze.

*Maps to: B1 / Setup — polar H₂O ready to attract other waters.*

### Q2. Hydrogen bonds

**Hydrogen bonds between water molecules are best described as:**

- [ ] Strong solid covalent bonds that permanently glue two waters into one molecule.  
- [x] **Weaker attractions (often shown as dashed lines) between neighboring H₂O molecules.**  
- [ ] Forces that only exist inside a single water molecule between O and H.  
- [ ] The same as ionic bonds between sodium and chloride.

*Maps to: B2 teach-back — dashed attractions, weaker than covalent but strong in numbers.*

### Q3. Cohesion / surface tension

**Cohesion in water mainly means that:**

- [ ] Water molecules push apart so the surface stays flat.  
- [ ] Water dissolves all solutes equally.  
- [x] **Water molecules stick to each other (via hydrogen bonds), which can create surface tension like a stretchy film.**  
- [ ] Water only sticks to plant cell walls, not to other water molecules.

*Maps to: B3 / bridges — cohesion from H-bonds → surface film.*

### Q4. Water strider

**Why can a water strider stand on a pond without sinking (as in the mystery)?**

- [ ] Pond water is slightly frozen at the surface.  
- [ ] Water is simply denser than the insect’s legs, so it can’t sink.  
- [x] **Cohesion between water molecules makes the surface act like an elastic film (surface tension) that can hold a light insect.**  
- [ ] The strider’s legs are magnetic and repel water.

*Maps to: B3 correct option + Resolution recap.*

### Q5. Optional — xylem (stretch / syllabus depth)

**In plants, cohesion between water molecules also helps explain how water can form a continuous column in xylem. Which idea fits best?**

- [ ] Xylem pumps water with tiny motors unrelated to bonding.  
- [x] **Hydrogen-bond cohesion helps water molecules hold onto each other in a chain as water moves upward.**  
- [ ] Xylem only works because water is nonpolar.  
- [ ] Plants don’t use cohesion; only insects do.

*Maps to: design scope (Water A1.1 includes xylem); may be lightly taught or inferred — mark as optional / “stretch” in Forms if desired.*  
*Scoring note: If you want a strict 3/5 from core items only, score Q1–Q4 as the core set and treat Q5 separately — or keep all five and use design’s 3/5 bar on the full set.*

**Comprehension success (design):** players correctly answer **≥ 3 of 5**.
