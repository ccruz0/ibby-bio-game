# A2.2 Cell Structure: "What's Inside the Box?"

## Episode ID
`cell-structure-a2.2`

## Overview
A flagship episode exploring eukaryotic and prokaryotic cell structure. The student and Ibby discover a mysterious speck under the microscope that reveals itself as a living cell — a miniature "city" with compartments, walls, and specialized structures. Through three battles, players map eukaryotic organelles, compare prokaryote and eukaryote architectures, and unlock why compartmentalization gave eukaryotes an evolutionary advantage.

## Theme
**"What's Inside the Box?"** — the mystery of cell structure and compartmentalization.

## Battles

### Battle 1: Drag-Label
**Title**: "What are the parts of a eukaryotic cell?"

Players label six key structures on an eukaryotic cell diagram:
- Nucleus (DNA compartment)
- Mitochondria (energy)
- Endoplasmic reticulum (protein synthesis)
- Golgi apparatus (protein packaging)
- Ribosomes (protein makers)
- Cell membrane (boundary)

Each structure is a dropzone with tight positional constraints. Teach-back emphasizes that eukaryotic cells are **compartmentalized** — each organelle is membrane-bound.

### Battle 2: Match
**Title**: "Prokaryote vs Eukaryote — how do they differ?"

Players match prokaryotic features to eukaryotic equivalents:
1. Nucleoid ↔ Nucleus
2. 70S ribosomes ↔ 80S ribosomes
3. Cell wall (prokaryote) ↔ Membrane-bound organelles (eukaryote)

Teach-back highlights the key differences: prokaryotes lack a nucleus and organelles; eukaryotes have both. This structural difference enables eukaryotes to be larger and more complex.

### Battle 3: Scenario (Multiple Choice)
**Title**: "Why does compartmentalization matter?"

**Question**: Why did eukaryotic cells evolve membrane-bound organelles?

**Correct Option**: Separate compartments let different reactions happen at once without interfering — more complex chemistry, greater efficiency.

**Wrong Options**:
- Compartments protect DNA from damage (prokaryotes less protected)
- Compartments allow larger cells (prokaryotes stay small)

Teach-back explains that compartmentalization enables **specialization and chemical efficiency** — a key evolutionary advantage.
